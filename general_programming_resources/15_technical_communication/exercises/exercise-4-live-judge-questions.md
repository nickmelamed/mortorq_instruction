# Exercise 4: Live Judge Questions

## Goal
Practice the abstraction dial live, out loud, in response to a question you didn't get to prepare for.

## Scenario
Everything else in this module is a written artifact you can draft and redraft. A judging interview isn't: a judge asks a follow-up, and you answer in the next few seconds, at whatever abstraction level actually answers what they just asked. Find a partner. One of you plays the judge, the other answers as the team member; you'll swap.

## Part A — The Climber Decision

The "judge" reads these four questions aloud, one at a time, waiting for a full answer before moving to the next. Don't show the team member the questions in advance.

1. "Tell me about one technical decision your team made this season."
2. "Why half an inch, and not something tighter or looser?"
3. "How does the code actually check that?"
4. "What would happen if the climber's target height needed to change mid-match?"

The team member answers using the climber tolerance decision from `concept.md`, live:

- Question 1 should get the **judge-level** answer: no code, no constant name, just the deliberate tradeoff (`concept.md`'s own "for a mentor or judge" version is the shape to aim for).
- Question 2 is asking for one notch more specificity than question 1 got; a real justification (encoder noise, or a plausible reason), still no code.
- Question 3 is the only question that's actually asking for the programmer-level detail; this is the moment to say the exact check and the constant, not before.
- Question 4 is a curveball that this module's example was never written to answer. A good answer says so honestly and reasons about it out loud, instead of inventing a confident-sounding fabrication.

Swap: the judge becomes the team member, the team member becomes the judge, and run the same four questions again.

## Part B — Your Own Decision

Pick a real technical decision, either from our existing robot or a hypothetical scenario. The judge invents their own follow-up questions this time, live, based on whatever the team member's first answer actually says (a real judge doesn't work from a script). Run at least three follow-ups per person, then swap.

## Self-Check
- [ ] My answer to question 1 (or the equivalent opening question in Part B) led with the conclusion, with zero code or config names in it
- [ ] I didn't volunteer the exact technical detail until a question actually asked for it
- [ ] When question 4 (or an equivalent curveball in Part B) hit something my answer couldn't actually support, I said so honestly instead of inventing an answer
- [ ] As the judge, I can say whether my partner's escalating answers actually matched what I asked, or whether they recited the same paragraph regardless of the question
- [ ] I did this out loud, live, with a partner. It was not written down and revised first

## Reflection
The written exercises in this module all let you notice you got the abstraction level wrong and fix it before anyone sees it. A live follow-up doesn't give you that. That pressure is exactly why this is worth rehearsing separately from writing: the skill is the same dial from the rest of this module, but reacting to it in real time, under a few seconds of thinking time, is a different muscle than revising a paragraph you can read back to yourself first.

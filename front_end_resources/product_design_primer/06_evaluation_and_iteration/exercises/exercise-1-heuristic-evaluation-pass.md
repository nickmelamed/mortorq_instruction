# Exercise 1: Run a Heuristic Evaluation Pass

## Goal
Find real problems using a checklist alone, before involving any other person.

## Steps
1. Pick a screen: your own hi-fi mockup carried through this primer, or an existing screen in `scouting_app`/`team_site`.
2. Walk through it against at least five of Nielsen's ten usability heuristics, including visibility of system status, match between system and the real world, user control and freedom, consistency and standards, and error prevention.
3. For every heuristic, write down whether the screen passes or fails, with a specific example either way — not a vague impression.
4. For every failure, assign a severity: cosmetic, minor, major, or blocker.
5. Sort your findings by severity, worst first.

## Self-Check
- [ ] I evaluated against at least five named heuristics, not a general impression
- [ ] Every finding names a specific element or interaction, not a vague "this feels off"
- [ ] Every failure has a severity rating
- [ ] My findings are sorted worst-first

## Reflection
Which heuristic caught something you wouldn't have noticed just from looking at the screen normally? What does that suggest about the value of a checklist versus relying on your own judgment alone?

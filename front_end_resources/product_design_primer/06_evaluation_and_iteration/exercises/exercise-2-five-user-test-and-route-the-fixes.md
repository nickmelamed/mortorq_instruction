# Exercise 2: Run a Small Usability Test, Then Route Each Finding Back

## Goal
Watch a real person attempt a real task, and practice deciding which earlier module actually owns the fix for what you find.

## Steps
1. Recruit one to three people who haven't seen your design. Give each one a single task ("log this match's result" or "find out when your kid's team plays next") and your wireframe, mockup, or the real app — say nothing else about how it works.
2. Watch without helping. Write down exactly where each person hesitated, misclicked, asked a question, or gave up.
3. Combine these findings with your heuristic evaluation results from Exercise 1 into one list.
4. For every finding on the combined list, name which module (`01` through `05`) you'd actually need to revisit to fix the root cause — not just which screen to patch.
5. Pick the single highest-severity finding and sketch, in one or two sentences, what you'd change and where.

## Self-Check
- [ ] I tested with at least one real person I didn't coach during the task
- [ ] I recorded specific hesitations/errors, not just a pass/fail impression
- [ ] Every finding, from both exercises, is routed to a specific earlier module
- [ ] I can state a concrete next step for the highest-severity finding

## Reflection
Did any finding trace all the way back to `01` — a needs statement that was wrong or incomplete, rather than a downstream screen being poorly built? If so, what does that suggest about how much confidence a later-stage polish pass can actually buy you if the foundational need was mis-stated to begin with?

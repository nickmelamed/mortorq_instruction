# Exercise 2: Check Legibility Under the Actual Condition It Needs to Survive

## Goal
Test a visual decision against the real constraint it's supposed to serve, instead of just checking it looks fine on a laptop indoors.

## Steps
1. Take your hi-fi screen from Exercise 1 and view it on an actual phone or tablet screen, outdoors, in direct sunlight (or the brightest light you can find if it's not sunny).
2. Try to read every piece of text and identify every button or control without shading the screen with your hand or body.
3. Note anything that becomes hard to read or find under those conditions that looked fine indoors.
4. Adjust the specific colors or contrast pairs responsible, and re-check outdoors.
5. Compare against the WCAG contrast numbers from Exercise 1 — note whether something that technically passed the numeric check still failed the real-world glare check, or vice versa.

## Self-Check
- [ ] I tested outdoors in real bright light, not just indoors with a brightness slider turned up
- [ ] I identified at least one specific element that was harder to read/find outdoors than indoors
- [ ] I made a concrete adjustment in response, not just a note for later
- [ ] I compared the numeric contrast check against the real-world result and can say whether they agreed

## Reflection
If the numeric contrast check and the outdoor test disagreed anywhere, which one would you trust more for this specific app, and why does that make sense given who's actually using it and where?

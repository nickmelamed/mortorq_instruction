# Exercise 1: Write Two Needs Statements

## Goal
Turn "I think users would like X" into a specific, testable sentence about a specific person, for both apps this curriculum builds.

## Steps
1. Pick one real user type of `scouting_app` (pit scout, drive coach, or strategist) and one of `team_site` (prospective member, parent, sponsor).
2. For at least one of the two, go watch someone who actually fits that role attempt the relevant task — logging a match, or finding information on the current team site — and write down exactly where they hesitated, mistyped, or got stuck. If you truly can't find a real person to observe, ask someone who fits the role what they were *actually trying to do* the last time they touched something similar (not what they'd want added).
3. Write a needs statement for each user, following the template in `concept.md`:
   `[Specific user], in [specific situation], needs to [specific action], because [specific consequence if they can't].`
4. For each statement, check: does it name a person and a situation, with zero mention of colors, layouts, or components? If a screen, button, or feature name snuck in, rewrite it out.

## Self-Check
- [ ] Both statements name a specific user and situation, not a general demographic
- [ ] Neither statement mentions a UI element, color, or feature by name
- [ ] At least one statement is grounded in an actual observation or direct conversation, not pure assumption
- [ ] I can read each statement back and say exactly what consequence follows if the need goes unmet

## Reflection
Reread your two statements. If you'd skipped the "why" step and just written down the first feature idea that came to mind instead, what would you have designed — and would it have actually solved the problem, or just resembled a solution?

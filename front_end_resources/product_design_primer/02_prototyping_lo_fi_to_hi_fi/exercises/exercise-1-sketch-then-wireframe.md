# Exercise 1: Sketch, Test, Then Wireframe

## Goal
Feel the difference between raising fidelity because a layer survived scrutiny versus raising it out of habit.

## Steps
1. Take a needs statement — your own from `01`, or this module's pick-list example — and draw a sketch of the screen it implies: boxes, arrows, labels, on paper or a whiteboard. No color, no real visual styling, and don't spend more than five minutes on it.
2. Show the sketch to one other person who hasn't seen it. Ask them to explain out loud what each part does and what they think would happen if they tapped or clicked on it. Don't explain anything yourself first.
3. Write down anywhere their explanation didn't match your intent.
4. Revise the sketch if the walkthrough surfaced a structural problem, and re-test if you made a real change.
5. Once the sketch survives a walkthrough without a structural correction, produce a wireframe: real proportions, real labels/content, grayscale only — still no color or type treatment.

## Self-Check
- [ ] I ran the walkthrough test before producing the wireframe, not after
- [ ] I can point to at least one thing that changed (or one thing that was confirmed correct) as a direct result of the test
- [ ] My wireframe has zero color and no type styling decisions in it — those are `04`'s job
- [ ] My wireframe settles real structural questions (what's a button vs. a link, what's on this screen vs. a different one)

## Reflection
If you'd skipped straight to a polished mockup instead of sketching first, and the walkthrough test had revealed the same structural problem, how would that have changed how willing you'd have felt to actually fix it?
